Why hang fruit above Ganpati?

A quick look at the tradition behind the canopy.

In Konkani and Goan households, the space above the Ganpati idol is never left bare. A wooden frame or a web of string is raised over the idol and dressed with whatever the land is giving that month — fruit, vegetables, forest berries, flowers and leaves. This hanging canopy is the Matoli.

It isn't decoration for its own sake. Ganpati is honoured as the provider who looks after the household, and the Matoli is the family's way of showing him the season's harvest before anyone else tastes it — and asking, in return, for an even better one next year.

Because everything on it comes from the backyard, the market, or the nearby forest, a Matoli also quietly keeps a record of local biodiversity: some homes still gather rare wild berries and leaves that would otherwise be forgotten between one Chaturthi and the next.
